The Predictive Robotic Mind

Why Robots Should Learn to Be Surprised

Can robots use experience not only to remember the past, but to form expectations about what happens next — and recognize when reality no longer matches them?

Catharina Helten · RPTU Kaiserslautern-Landau

Abstract

From experience to expectation

Humans adapt not only by perceiving the world, but also by comparing what they perceive with what they expected. When the world behaves differently than expected, we become cautious and adjust our actions. Autonomous robots still struggle with this kind of self-assessment: they can perceive their surroundings, but often cannot recognize when their interpretation may be uncertain.

This work proposes the Predictive Robotic Mind, a human-inspired concept for adaptive autonomy. It compares current observations with memory-based expectations and uses prediction error as an internal signal for behavior adaptation. The goal is to develop robots that not only detect their surroundings, but also recognize when the world no longer matches their expectations.

About the author
